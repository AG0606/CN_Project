## A. Great Britain: Janet plus NESO regional carbon

The [Internet Topology Zoo](https://github.com/mroughan/InternetTopologyZoo) publishes Janet Backbone as GraphML/GML and says its networks were transcribed from operator maps. The [specific GraphML](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mroughan/InternetTopologyZoo/main/graphml/Janetbackbone.graphml) is a March 2011 snapshot with node coordinates and link records; direct inspection found **29 nodes and 45 undirected edges**. Two nodes need removal for a strict Great Britain experiment: **Dublin** (Ireland) and **NIRAN** (Northern Ireland, outside NESO's GB carbon coverage). The remaining **27-node, 42-edge graph is connected**; a smaller connected, cyclic core could be selected for a laptop-scale replay. Link capacity and delay should be declared modelling assumptions unless separately verified, because this GraphML does not provide numeric capacities. The Topology Zoo repository carries a [CC BY 4.0 licence](https://github.com/mroughan/InternetTopologyZoo/blob/main/LICENSE) and requests citation of [Knight et al., *The Internet Topology Zoo*](https://github.com/mroughan/InternetTopologyZoo#citation).

[NESO's official Carbon Intensity API](https://api.carbonintensity.org.uk/) provides historical queries for its **14 GB distribution-network regions** and lists their IDs. NESO describes a **30-minute** regional model of carbon intensity *of electricity consumed*, with regional power-flow accounting; its [site](https://carbonintensity.org.uk/) says data start after 26 September 2017, allows regional CSV download in 30-day chunks, and licenses the API under **CC BY 4.0**. A direct check of the historical `/regional/intensity/{from}/{to}` response found `intensity.forecast` but no `intensity.actual` for regional entries. Thus call these **historical regional forecast/model estimates**, not measured regional CO₂. The national API separately offers estimated actual values. [NESO API reference](https://api.carbonintensity.org.uk/), [NESO methodology and data page](https://carbonintensity.org.uk/).

Geographic join: keep each Janet node's latitude/longitude, assign it to a **documented NESO region ID**, and store that lookup as a reviewed CSV. Because NESO regions follow electricity distribution boundaries rather than network boundaries, use a boundary map or location/postcode lookup for ambiguous sites; do not infer that a named network region is the same as a power-grid region. Exclude Dublin and Northern Ireland, or supply a separately sourced Irish/Northern Irish carbon series if those nodes must remain. [Topology graph](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mroughan/InternetTopologyZoo/main/graphml/Janetbackbone.graphml), [NESO region IDs and postcode endpoint](https://api.carbonintensity.org.uk/), [NESO region-boundary explanation](https://carbonintensity.org.uk/).

**Small-subgraph feasibility check:** A connected eight-site core can retain **Glasgow, Edinburgh, Newcastle, Warrington, Leeds, Reading, Bristol, and London**, with ten links and alternative routes. A direct postcode-API check mapped illustrative city-centre outward postcodes to NESO regions: G1→2, EH1→2, NE1→4, WA1→6, LS1→5, RG1→12, BS1→11, and SW1A→13. This yields six distinct regional carbon series. These postcodes locate cities rather than confirmed router premises, so verify the site-to-region assignments before publication. [Original Janet graph](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sk2/topologyzoo/master/sources/Janetbackbone.graphml), [NESO postcode endpoint](https://api.carbonintensity.org.uk/).

**Traffic caveat:** The Topology Zoo file supplies a graph, **not** traffic matrices. Jisc's [Netsight](https://www.jisc.ac.uk/netsight) offers connection-level monitoring to Janet-connected organisations through accounts, not an openly downloadable backbone OD matrix. A synthetic OD generator with fixed seeds and declared daily cycles is the honest low-friction choice. If desired, calibrate its total rate against public network statistics only where definitions match. State in the paper: *real, geographically located national network topology + real NESO regional carbon estimates + simulated traffic demand*. Also state that combining a 2011 topology with 2017-or-later carbon is a **counterfactual replay**, not a contemporaneous historical observation.

## B. US: Abilene traffic plus same-year annual carbon

Abilene offers the strongest free national **measured OD traffic** candidate. The [original collector's readme](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~yzhang/research/AbileneTM/readme.txt) describes 24 non-contiguous weeks in 2004, **12 × 12 OD entries every five minutes**, and distinguishes the `realOD` field from four estimation fields. The rate units are `100 bytes / 5 minutes` because of 1/100 packet sampling; the readme also notes excluded traffic destined for Abilene's own AS. The original [tar archive responds publicly](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~yzhang/research/AbileneTM/AbileneTM-all.tar) (about 178 MB), but an explicit redistribution licence was not located; cite the collector and avoid committing raw copies without checking terms. [Topology Zoo's Abilene snapshot](https://topology-zoo.org/dataset.html) is dated February 2005, so confirm that graph/OD labels and links agree with the **2004 routing matrix `A`** supplied with the trace before replaying it. [Collector readme](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~yzhang/research/AbileneTM/readme.txt), [Topology Zoo catalogue](https://topology-zoo.org/dataset.html).

The US EPA's [historical eGRID archive](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/historical-egrid-data) includes **2004** regional power-sector CO₂ *output emission rates* in lb/MWh; EPA explains the rates and geographic caveats in its [eGRID FAQ](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/frequent-questions-about-egrid). This solves the **year** mismatch and gives geographic differences, but annual average rates are fixed through each simulated day. They describe power generation in a region, not the instantaneous electricity consumed at each router, and do not support a claim that routing followed hourly grid changes. City-to-eGRID-region lookup needs documented assumptions because EPA says eGRID regions are not always strict geographic polygons. [EPA FAQ](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/frequent-questions-about-egrid).

## C. US: Abilene traffic plus modern hourly carbon

EIA's [Hourly Electric Grid Monitor](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/930-content/about.html) publishes hourly demand and estimated CO₂ emissions by US balancing authority; CO₂ series start **July 2018**, and historical data are downloadable in regional files. EIA warns of reporting and interchange-model quality limits. Its [API is free but requires registration/key](https://www.eia.gov/opendata/v1/); downloadable CSV/XLSX files are an alternative. A router city may lie near or within a complicated balancing-authority service area, so mapping a city to a grid area is an approximation that should be documented, not inferred from state alone. Pairing these values with 2004 Abilene traffic is again **scenario replay**, never measured contemporaneous traffic/carbon. [EIA-930 data notes](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/930-content/about.html), [EIA open-data terms](https://www.eia.gov/opendata/v1/).

## Recommendation for this project

Keep **GÉANT as the main experiment** and use **Option B, Abilene + eGRID2004**, for a second within-one-country evaluation. This follows the team's preference for measured-derived traffic rather than generated OD demand. Use the trace's `realOD` field, verify its node ordering and routing matrix against the chosen 2004-era Abilene topology, map each retained US node to an EPA eGRID region, and apply that region's 2004 annual CO₂ output rate as a spatially varying but **time-constant** carbon input. Retrain the traffic model on Abilene's chronological training split; do not reuse a GÉANT checkpoint. Run the same routing policies and report within-Abilene paired differences, rather than comparing raw scores across topologies. Existing code assumes eight nodes and 15-minute bins, so supporting Abilene's 12 nodes and five-minute source cadence requires a deliberate data/model generalization or a documented resampling and connected-subgraph choice. [Abilene collector readme](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~yzhang/research/AbileneTM/readme.txt), [EPA historical eGRID](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/historical-egrid-data), [EPA rate definitions](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/frequent-questions-about-egrid).

This design answers the **geographic** question and preserves empirical traffic. It does **not** test reactions to changing grid conditions: annual eGRID rates do not change during replay. For that separate question, a later sensitivity could pair Abilene with [EIA's 2018-onward hourly regional carbon data](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/930-content/about.html), clearly labelled a cross-era counterfactual. Janet + NESO remains an alternative only if the team later accepts synthetic traffic. In every option, the project's forwarding-carbon score remains a **routing proxy**, not grams of network CO₂; a calibrated device power model would be needed for physical emission estimates.
