# Research note: improving carbon-aware traffic engineering

27 October 2026. This note suggests experiments; none has been run in this project. No method guarantees a significant improvement on our traces.

## What our results already imply

The frozen follow-up has modest Abilene carbon-score improvements, mixed overload and increased route changes; more training did not consistently improve forecasting. Its GÉANT later-period replay has three days with no listed native demands, plus severe saturation under the fixed assumed capacities. These limitations are documented in [the follow-up study](../FOLLOWUP_STUDY.md). A new method should target the routing decision and the evaluation design, not presume that a larger predictor solves the problem.

## 1. Service-first joint optimization (recommended first)

For the current single-path candidates, compare route assignments lexicographically: (1) minimize predicted unserved demand, computed by applying the same capacity-sharing rule as the simulator; (2) among routes with approximately the same predicted service, minimize predicted carbon attribution; (3) penalize route changes and enforce the existing path-stretch limit. If capacity cannot carry all offered demand, explicitly optimize the service objective rather than treating an impossible peak-utilization guard as a yes/no condition. A tunable service tolerance belongs to validation only. Compare this against the current joint planner, reactive carbon, shortest path, and a **service-first planner with the same persistence forecast**. Validate both pooled served bytes and per-flow service/fairness, carbon score, absolute carbon-weighted forwarded bytes, overload, latency proxy and churn. Since the carbon score is normalized per served byte, a drop in it alone could accompany worse service or a changed traffic mix.

This is an inference from the present implementation and traffic-engineering principles, not a published result for our system. It requires no new neural training and preserves the same demand, candidates and capacities. Planning on predictions cannot guarantee actual service. Use the previous test only as development data after its outcomes were inspected, and freeze the choice before a fresh final test.

## 2. Multipath routing with service and carbon optimization

Instead of assigning each origin–destination aggregate to a single path, let a central controller distribute it over a small set of candidate paths. A continuous linear program can first maximize served demand subject to link capacities, then minimize carbon-weighted forwarding subject to maintaining the first-stage service and stretch constraints. Add a limit on changes in splitting ratios. Explicitly label the result an **aggregate fractional multipath simulation**: splitting one OD aggregate is plausible when it contains many independent flows, but is not evidence that a single flow can be arbitrarily split without packet reordering. Include unsplit baselines and a multipath service-only baseline so the value of carbon-awareness is measurable.

This is a substantive simulator extension: path fractions, route-change accounting, fairness, fractional service, conservation, link-capacity constraints and score calculations all need to be updated and tested for **every compared policy**. A prediction-time LP must choose splits using historical/forecast data; realize service later using actual traffic with a separately specified common allocation model, never current-bin future demand. Equal input traces and candidate path sets are necessary. A test with few large indivisible flows or quantized split weights should expose where ideal fractional results are optimistic. If both candidates share a bottleneck, if offered load exceeds a network cut, or the forecast is wrong, multipath cannot remove that structural limitation.

The [SMORE paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/nsdi18/nsdi18-kumar.pdf) reports that diverse paths and adaptive path weights improved congestion and robustness in its tested networks, and emphasizes path-count, churn and hardware constraints. [Google's Niagara](https://research.google/pubs/efficient-traffic-splitting-on-commodity-switches/) investigates implementable SDN splitting on commodity switches. These results motivate the technique; neither proves its benefit for our six pairs or carbon metric. Fractional splitting is not itself novel.

## 3. Scenario-based uncertainty for the service-first planner

Build a small set of demand scenarios from the latest observable demand, GNN forecast, and forecast residuals fitted on validation data only; preserve cross-flow correlations by sampling residual vectors or past matrices, and ensure nonnegative values. Choose a single assignment against expected service and a tail-risk criterion across scenarios, then break ties with carbon cost and churn. Compare equal-budget versions driven by GNN, persistence and a no-margin forecast, plus the existing scalar-margin planner. Report empirical coverage and risk on new periods; avoid calling it a formal reliability guarantee without justified assumptions.

[COPE](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/papers/COPE.pdf) has already studied expected-case traffic engineering with robustness to unexpected demand, including Abilene traces. Thus robust demand-aware TE is established work. A practical contribution here could be its combination with coarse regional carbon data and carbon–service tradeoffs, if comparisons show something reproducible. Overly conservative scenarios can increase detours or carbon cost, and training distribution shift can make historical residuals misleading.

## 4. Decision-focused prediction (later, more effort)

Instead of training only to minimize traffic-matrix error, train toward a routing objective, such as the regret in service and carbon relative to the best decision available under the same constraints. [DOTE](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/nsdi23-perry.pdf) directly learns traffic-engineering decisions from historical demand, reporting strong WAN results in its own setting. This supports studying decision quality rather than MAE alone; it does not establish a carbon-specific or GNN advantage here. An exact differentiable treatment of the current discrete assignment and max-min service is nontrivial, and a surrogate must not be called end-to-end optimization without verification. Because the current link-load proxy loss did not consistently help, this is a higher-risk follow-up after routing changes and baselines.

## Scientific design and claims

- Before another test, specify source-availability rules and multiple capacity/load regimes from training information or documented assumptions. Preserve the completed frozen study; do not replace its test days after seeing outcomes. Use genuinely untouched later days or clearly label any reused test period exploratory. Treat missing native demand sections as unknown source availability, not proof of real zero traffic.
- Freeze policies, weights, splits and operating regimes before evaluation. Compare identical timestamps, forecasts, carbon signals, candidate paths and assumed capacities; use multiple days and model seeds and report paired daily differences and uncertainty. If capacities or traffic scale change, rerun *all* methods under the same changes; they are scenario assumptions, not measured historical capacity or operational performance.
- Distinguish forwarding carbon attribution from measured emissions. The [CATE study](https://doi.org/10.1145/3629165) emphasizes that real carbon benefits depend on equipment power behavior, and warns that there is no universally effective technique. Our Abilene carbon signal is annual by region, so it supports spatial comparisons, not claims about hourly carbon shifting. GÉANT's traffic and carbon years differ.
- Service-first optimization, multipath, robust scenarios and decision-focused learning are established separately. A claim of a novel combination or finding needs a broader literature review and relevant matched baselines. A larger numerical percentage from a changed workload, carbon normalization or capacity does not by itself establish improvement.
